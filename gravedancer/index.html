Gravedancer

Hold. Snap. Flow.

A graveyard warden who fights skeletons with a funeral-rite blade dance, built on Roblox's standard 15-part R15 body.

This project is about the parts of a character you feel more than see. It has a full control rig in Blender that bakes down to the 15 R15 parts, hand-keyed game animation, a live bridge that sends clips from Blender straight into Roblox Studio, and a Luau animation system with layers, masks, markers and procedural IK.

Animation

A motion style before a single key

Before animating, I picked an archetype whose motion would show timing. The Gravedancer moves like a mourner: long, quiet holds, then a sudden cut, then a slow follow-through. That contrast shows off timing, arcs and overlap, and it works within R15's limits.

Motion rules

  1. Hold → snap → flow. A calm hold, a cut that crosses its arc in 2–3 frames, then a long, smooth unwind. Never one even speed.
  2. Spin, don't step. Turns pivot on the ball of the planted foot. The chest leads the hips by 2–3 frames, then the hips catch up.
  3. Two swords, never in unison. When one cuts, the other trails or holds a guard.
  4. Wide arcs. The blade tip travels on a smooth curve, checked with motion paths. No straight jabs.
  5. Long follow-through. The body unwinds about 5° past the stance and settles back; the off-hand sword and head lag 1–2 frames.
  6. Light and upright. Hips stay high, and he lands softly on the ball of the foot.

R15 can't key scale, so the style has to come from poses, timing contrast, arcs and trails alone.

How the idle was built

  • The warden at rest: upright and still, head slightly bowed, as if keeping watch over the graves.
  • A seamless 40-frame loop built with Cycles modifiers. Keys are offset per channel, so the body parts overlap instead of moving together.
  • Every looped curve's first key equals its last, and the clip range matches the cycle exactly. A longer range would make the wrist jump about 0.4 studs at the wrap.
  • Pulled into Studio with my bridge, saved to Roblox, and set as the zero-speed sample of the locomotion blend space.
  • The combat idle (swords drawn, 72 frames) is being blocked now.

Planned moveset

Calm and combat idles, walk and run in both modes, start, stop and 180° pivot, leans, draw and sheath, a 3-hit combo, a charge-and-dash special, a dodge roll and an additive hit react. Every clip has its frame count, key poses and marker frames written down before I animate it.

Rigging

A production rig for a 15-part body

R15 has no extra deform bones and Roblox can't key scale. So everything an animator expects (IK, foot roll, space switching, stretch) has to live in helper bones and bake down to the same 15 parts plus the root.

Controls

  • IK/FK on every limb, blended by a keyable slider, with snap buttons both ways that don't pop.
  • Reverse foot: heel, toe and ball pivots. The R15 legs are perfectly straight, so the IK chain is pre-bent 0.001 studs to give the solver a direction.
  • Torso: root → centre of gravity → hips and chest as siblings, so each can twist on its own. A waist_twist slider shares part of the chest twist with the hips.
  • Head isolation from 0 (follows the chest) to 1 (keeps its world direction).
  • Hand spaces: world, hips, chest, head, plus a prop space for two-handed grips.

Working around R15

  • Stretch by translation: past full reach, the elbow or knee gap opens by half the overshoot and the wrist or ankle by the full amount.
  • Generic prop slots instead of a sword bone, so the same rig and clips work with any weapon or with none.
  • Custom control shapes ("Studwork"), modelled at real size and fitted to each R15 block. Left is red, right is blue, centre is yellow.
  • Locked channels everywhere scale or the wrong axis could slip into a clip.
  • Animation lives in separate files that link the rig through a library override, so rig fixes reach every clip.
Pipeline and runtime

Blender to Studio without FBX

Studio can't export a rig as FBX, and FBX brings axis and scale problems. So I built a bridge: a Blender add-on and a Studio plugin that talk over localhost and send joint transforms directly.

Blender control rig→bake to 16 bones→Start Bridge→Studio: Pull Animation→Save to Roblox→AnimConfig id

Bridge

  • Pose markers named Name:Value become Roblox keyframe markers: HitStart, ComboWindow, Footstep:Left, Draw:Right.
  • Root motion on the HumanoidRootPart is folded into the Root joint, so what I see in Blender is what plays in Studio.
  • A round-trip test compares a pose in Blender with the same pose pulled into Studio.

Luau animation system

  • A state machine with a 2D blend space for locomotion, plus full-body and upper-body tracks for attacks while running.
  • Upper-body mask, tested in Studio: the root and hip poses are weight-0 containers, so locomotion keeps the legs. My test readings showed the waist and shoulders at 1 and the root and hips at 0.
  • Additive layers and procedural foot IK applied as a post-pass on the joints.
  • A holster script welds the swords to the back or the hands, swapping them on Draw / Sheath markers.
  • A debug HUD (F1), modelled on Unreal's Animation Insights, lists every playing track with its priority, weight, speed and time, plus recent markers and transitions.
Characters

The Gravedancer and the Skeleton

Both use Roblox's R15 Block body with rigid outfit pieces welded to each body part, coloured from a palette and with no textures. Every animation works on the stock body, and the only uploads are meshes.

Concept

AI concept images made in Google Flow, used as a target for the character and the swords. I picked the curved, hollow sword with a glowing rim (top left) to build.
