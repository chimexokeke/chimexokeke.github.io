Be Still

Metsä katsoo. The forest is watching.

A winter night in a Finnish forest. Your torch dies, and eyes open between the trees. If you stop walking, a fire fox comes out of the dark to meet you. Move, and it's gone.

Play on itch.io →

The whole game uses one control. Hold to walk, move the mouse to look, and let go to stand still. I modelled, textured, rigged and animated the fox, and I made the shaders that give it fur and fire and the behaviour that decides how close it dares to come.

The encounter

Trust is the only mechanic

The fox approaches in stages, and walking at any point before it trusts you makes it bolt. It comes back warier each time. Once it trusts you, your body stays still to the end and only your gaze follows it.

Folklore, read loosely

In Finnish belief, a forest's haltija could take an animal's form, and the northern lights are revontulet, "fox fires", sparks thrown up by a fox's tail. In this telling, the forest chose the fire fox. Be Still is my own interpretation of these stories, not a retelling.

Behaviour as a state machine

Hidden → Cross → Freeze → Curious → Turn to face → Approach (stand, walk, trot, freeze, sniff…) → Trust → Brush → Sit → Play, with a startle (freeze, then run into the dark) from every stage before Trust. Each state picks its clip and its movement speed, and the camera's gaze drifts toward the fox's face more strongly as trust grows.

Character

Modelling, texturing, rigging and animating the fox

I modelled the fox as a game-ready mesh, unwrapped and textured it, then built a lean custom rig instead of a general-purpose auto-rig: cheap enough for the browser, complete enough to animate.

Rig

  • 107 bones, 56 of them deform bones, skinned at 4 influences for Unity.
  • Legs: digitigrade IK with paw squares and heel roll, then flex, tip and heel controls plus an auto-heel slider. I added these after the first animation pass showed the paws were too limited.
  • The 2.9 m mythical tail (the body is a real fox's size) has a master control plus additive per-bone controls, so big arcs and small flicks stay separate.
  • A look target with a blend slider lets the head track the player without counter-animating.

Animation

  • 19 hand-keyed clips: stand idle, walk, trot, run, freeze and hold, curious, a sniff split into start, loop and end, turn to face, tail wag, leg brush, settle and sitting idle, pounce and play. See them below.
  • The game moves the fox at each clip's measured stride speed, so the paws stay planted. Pounce and play run on the spot in Blender; the game carries the fox over the snow at a speed measured from its planted paws, frame by frame.
  • The turn to face the visitor is my keyed turn: the game reads it off the root bone each frame and only scales it to land three-quarter on.
  • Every clip keys the full control set, so no pose leaks from one clip into the next during export.
Animation

From reference to Blender to the game

After studying how foxes move, especially foxes in snow, I gathered reference footage, animated the fox's behaviour by hand in Blender, and implemented it in Unity, where a behaviour script decides which clip plays and how the fox moves through the forest.

Each row shows a reference I studied, my clip in Blender on the game mesh, and the same moment in the game, filmed from a low angle. The reference clips are other people's footage (mostly BBC wildlife film), shown here only as study material.

Walking in snow

A fox in deep snow lifts its paws high and places them almost in a line, its head low and steady while the body rolls over the legs. The walk and trot keep that economy, and the long mythical tail floats behind on its own delay.

Freeze, then curious

Wild foxes stop dead the moment they notice you, then study you with small head tilts and ear flicks. Off the trot the fox freezes, holds the freeze, then turns curious, with a procedural head and eye aim keeping its gaze on the visitor wherever they stand.

Deciding: the turn to face you

A short, careful turn in three or four steps, the weight shifting before each paw moves. I keyed the turn on a fake root in Blender, the export bakes it onto the real root, and the game takes it off the root bone each frame, so the turn you see is the one I animated.

Trust: sniff, rub, sit

Close up, the fox sniffs the air and your boots, rubs its flank against your leg, then turns back to you and sits. The sniff is split into start, loop and end, so the game can hold it for as long as a beat needs.

The mousing pounce

Foxes hunting under snow sprint, hop twice to listen, then dive head first and bury themselves up to the tail. The game measures the clip in Blender (how far the paws travel, and when the body goes under the snow) and throws up the snow on that frame. While the tail sweeps the snow, sparks rise from it and build the aurora.

Play: the belly flop

The same sprint and double hop, but it lands flat on its belly and wiggles, sweeping the snow with its tail, then gets up. The bear, elk, deer and swan come out of the dark to watch and play.

World

Spirit animals and the winter forest

When the fox trusts you, a bear, a deer, an elk and a swan step out of the dark to play with it. They're shown here next to the fox for scale. The forest is built from a small low-poly kit that the game streams along an endless path.

Environment props

Pines, young pines, shrubs, rocks and stumps, flat-shaded with snow baked into the vertex colours, so the whole forest shares one shader and no textures.

Technical art

Soft fur and a tail of fire, in a browser

  • Shell fur: a custom URP shader draws the fur as stacked shells, with strand locks and coat lag driven by the body's motion. Colour and fur direction come from maps baked per pixel from the mesh and bone masks, so the markings don't show the low-poly surface.
  • Auto quality: 32 layers on PC; 10 in the browser, dropping to 6 when the camera is close to the head, then render scale if the frame rate stays low. A fur test scene (fox and ground only) ran at about 45 fps on Intel UHD integrated graphics at 1080p.
  • Flame tail: 70 crossed-ribbon flame tongues, skinned to the tail bones and rendered additively. They fade out near the camera so they never streak across the view.
  • Awakening: one value drives a colour wave through the fur, the embers turning into aurora curtains, and a colour grade blending from deep winter night (kaamos) to awakened.
  • Night forest: an endless path streamed in 16 m chunks, snow and pine-needle shaders, fog that closes in when the torch dies, and 20 pairs of eyes that open when you stop.
