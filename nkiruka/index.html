Nkiruka "Stillwing"

The pain shows in her body, never her face.

A former dancer whose left leg was crushed, who now walks with a cane. Can someone in her condition still fight?

I wrote the character first, then filmed myself as reference for every move and keyframed her in Maya. Each move had to answer the same questions: where is her weight, and how does she keep it off the bad knee? The clips are set up in Unreal Engine with Blueprints.

Character

A dancer relearning every move

Before animating anything I wrote a character profile, so that every animation would show her backstory and attitude.

Who she is

  • Nkiru grew up in a travelling performance troupe and was famous for dancing with her fiancé.
  • Raiders attacked a show. Her fiancé died shielding her, and her left leg was crushed. The townsfolk started calling her Stillwing: a bird that no longer flies.
  • She seems cold and keeps a plain, unreadable face. She hides her pain behind calm, controlled actions.
  • She fights with her late fiancé's dagger. Her cane, made by the troupe's carpenter and held in her right hand, takes weight off her left leg. In a fight it blocks and strikes low.

How she fights

In short, brutal bursts. A hard cane strike breaks the enemy's balance, then fast dagger work finishes them before they recover.

Her weakness drives the animation: the cane is also the only thing keeping her upright. Losing balance costs her far more than it costs other fighters, so every attack has to end with the cane planted again.

Research and reference

I got the cane wrong first

I shot reference of myself with a crutch for each move. In the first pass I held it on the same side as the injured leg, with the bad leg on the right. My mentors pointed out that this isn't how people use a cane or crutch.

The rule: the cane goes opposite the injured leg

The cane is held in the hand on the strong side and moves forward together with the injured leg. Each time the bad leg is on the ground, the cane is on the ground too, on the other side of the body. That spreads her weight across a wider base and moves it away from the bad knee. With the cane on the same side, all the weight stays over the injured leg, so the cane barely helps.

The fix was to move the injury to her left knee, keep the cane in her right hand and the dagger in her left, and shoot new reference.

Before
Cane on the same side as the bad leg

I filmed the idle and walk this way, then animated both before the mistake was caught.

After
Cane in the right hand, injured left knee

What I watched for in every clip

  • Weight: the hips sit over the right leg, and the left foot touches lightly and leaves early.
  • Timing: the left leg gets a short, guarded contact, and the right leg a long, loaded one. That uneven rhythm is the limp.
  • The cane takes the load: the right shoulder dips and the elbow locks when it plants.
  • A calm face: per the profile, the effort shows in the body and never in her expression, even mid-combo.

Process

Character profile→Film reference→Block in Maya→Mentor review→Polish→Export FBX→Unreal + Blueprints

Every clip was playblasted from front, side and back cameras, so a lean or weight shift that reads well from one angle couldn't hide a problem in another.

Playing in Unreal Engine
