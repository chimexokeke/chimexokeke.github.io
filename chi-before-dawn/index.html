CHI: Before Dawn

A mother prayed for her dying son. Chi, his guardian angel, carries that prayer to the gods in a calabash that holds the boy's life, and crows try to stop him before dawn. Tap a crow to sling it.

I built this playable ad for my own game: the art and animation was done in Blender, the gameplay and game feel in TypeScript and Three.js, and one HTML file that a phone can run.

The loop

How a run plays

The run is paced by kills, not a fixed timeline, so a slow player still sees a full story. A small sun timer counts down to dawn, and the sky brightens as it runs.

Frames are captured straight from the renderer; the HUD and text are HTML over the canvas and aren't in these stills. Play the ad above to see them.

Art and asset pipeline

Blender → GLB → one HTML file

Every step is a script I can rerun on its own: Blender exports, a texture export, a pack step and the build. The build prints a size report and fails above 5 MB.

Chi: modelled, UV unwrapped, rigged and animated by me

I created Chi in Blender, from the detailed sculpt to the game-ready mesh, then UV unwrapped it, rigged it and animated it. Every clip the ad plays is my own animation. The headdress feathers, bead strands, loincloth and calabash all have their own bones so they swing with the motion.

Gameplay animation

A few clips from Chi's in-game animation set, from the same Blender file I export to the engine. Chi flies through the whole game, so every action is built on top of the swim pose, and the calabash, beads and tail keep moving after the body stops. Rendered in clay so the motion is easy to read.

Chi, the hero

  • I modelled and animated the character and props in Blender and exported the GLB files. The first GLB was 73 MB because of an 8K normal map; without it Chi was 0.28 MB.
  • Textured with a flat colour block-out, an AO bake, and a procedural pass driven by baked position and normal maps, so the uli patterns cross UV seams.
  • Clip merging: glTF stores a channel, sampler and two accessors per bone property per clip (10,464 channels). My pack script lays all clips on one timeline, drops tracks that never move and folds constant ones into the rest pose: 325 channels left, and the clips are cut back out at load time. 4.1 MB → 496 KB.
  • Later size pass: only the 5 clips the ad plays, and the mesh decimated to 60% (33k → 20k triangles) with no seam tearing: 374 KB.

Crow

Modelled and rigged in Blender: 274 triangles, flat shaded, ember eyes, Flap / Glide / Dive / Caw. The boss is the same mesh ×3.9 with a crest. 20 KB packed. Passes a 40 px silhouette test against the sky.

Village kit

The village prop were also modeled in blender. Ten props in one flat palette texture (20 KB, no alpha), 1,230 triangles, 60 KB packed. Placed by an authored layout in code with instancing: a winding path, river and bridge, a market, walled compounds, fields and a sacred grove.

Built in code

Sky with the sun in one shader, parallax hills, terrain with vertex colours, path and river ribbons, pebbles, smoke, and 57 villagers and 7 dogs animated procedurally in 8 draw calls. No new textures.

Low-poly environment assets

I also modelled the low-poly assets used in the game: round and rectangular houses, the obi, a yam barn, compound walls, a clay pot, an ekwe drum, yam stacks, palms, plantain, shrubs, grasses, rocks and trees. Most are under 100 triangles, with textured and flat-palette versions.

Gameplay and game feel

Code as a creative tool

  • Hit-stop: game time freezes 60 ms per kill, 100 ms per boss hit, 160 ms on the boss kill. Real-time systems keep running.
  • Trauma shake: offset grows with trauma², from layered sines plus a little roll, applied just before render so the camera follow stays clean.
  • Particles: two pooled systems (feathers, embers), 2 draw calls. Bursts inherit 70% of the flight velocity so they travel with the action. Motes of light fly from each kill into the calabash.
  • Readable threats: the crow's caw and a dark orb at its beak are the window to answer. Taps pick the nearer target, crow or cloud, with a generous touch radius and aim assist.
  • Audio: 11 sounds, decoded at boot so the first tap has sound; pitch jitter, rate-limited screeches, a heartbeat when critical, music ducking at dawn.
  • Before/after: ?juice=0 turns off hit-stop, shake, particles, the crack shader and sound, for side-by-side comparison.

Tuned by play, not by the plan

I first planned a 25–30 s rush. Playtesting changed it: a calm ~60 s run paced by kills (12 normal crows, 8 shield crows, then a breather and the boss), no slow-motion interruptions after the first tap, and PLAY NOW fading in over 2.8 s once time is up or the boss falls.

A swipe-to-dodge verb was built, tested and removed because it diluted the one-tap idea. The answer to a crow's attack became "shoot first", and later "shoot the cloud down", which kept one verb and added skill.

A test bot (?bot=0.5) plays at a set tap rate, so every pacing change was checked for fast, slow and idle players.

Minimal, Igbo-inspired UI

The HUD is one small calabash with a flame for the boy's life: it shrinks, turns coral and beats faster as he weakens. Short words are carved uli art generated, cut out and cleaned up as tiny WebPs (about 5 KB each); sentences sit on a carved plank. Banners are see-through so crows show behind them.

Mobile performance

Measured, then cut

  • All materials converted to Lambert: no PBR, much cheaper per pixel on phones.
  • An additive glow sprite stands in for bloom; no post-processing.
  • Resolution scaler: DPR capped at 2 and stepped down when frames stay above 20 ms.
  • Props, pebbles and villagers are instanced; the whole ground is one decal mesh.
  • Debug build only: lil-gui panel, fps/draw-call/triangle readout, free camera, grayscale value check. All stripped from the release by a compile-time flag.
